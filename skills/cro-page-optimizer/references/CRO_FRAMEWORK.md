# CRO Page Optimizer: public six-gate review framework

This is a generalized original framework informed by CRO practice and the Hormozi Value Equation. It is not a copy of proprietary competitor tools, client-specific SEO/CRO data or CEO Creative commercial terms.

## Classification before analysis

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| OBSERVED | Verbatim screenshot/source/code/analytics behavior actually supplied or seen |
| SOURCED | Independently verified external fact with URL and date |
| INFERRED | Reasoned interpretation of observed evidence, not a measured effect |
| SYNTHETIC | Illustrative scenario that must never be attributed to real buyers |
| UNKNOWN | Missing/insufficient evidence; do not substitute zero |
| NOT ASSESSED | Relevant, but cannot be reviewed without the source/page/tool |
| NOT APPLICABLE | The conversion surface or channel genuinely lacks that feature |

Source classification and publication status are **separate**: see `<plugin_root>/references/evidence-contract.md`. A factual claim can only be `verified` or `qualified` if properly supported and within its original limits.

## Gate 1: intent, context, and search

Identify channel and user objective before touching headlines. For organic, use supplied Search Console queries rather than guessing; test whether H1/title/intro/FAQ match task intent. Preserve canonical URL and existing structured metadata. For paid email/social, match the originating promise and destination. Flag intent collisions and redundant CTAs. A CRO audit without SEO intent is fine when organic search is irrelevant.

## Gate 2: offer / value equation / commercial clarity

Ask WHAT, WHO, WHY, HOW as a stranger's three-second test. Value Equation: (desired outcome × perceived likelihood) / (time delay × effort/sacrifice). Treat it as a qualitative decision framework, not a numerical performance estimator. Audit membership, renewal, cancellation, eligibility, deposits, due dates, guarantees, relevant costs, next action and fulfilment delays using official source and owner approval. Don't collapse a complex term into a misleading "free" or "risk-free".

## Gate 3: visual attention and first screen

One primary route: title -> buyer benefit -> clear next step -> compact trust/terms. Review rendered 320px, 390px, 768px and 1440px where actual screenshots can be obtained; note what was not rendered. Aim for body >=16px on phones; keyboard/focus accessible, no horizontal overflow, readable dark/light contrast, credible proof at decision points. Do not assume earlier field visibility equals a lift.

## Gate 4: actual journey friction

Map events to page stage and device:
- denominators: exposed visitors vs sessions vs clicks vs unique applicants;
- early friction: navigation backtracking, forced sign-in, field confusion, form errors and duplicate actions;
- technical failures: submission delays, frozen CTA, errors, lost state, redirects, unresponsive keyboard, mobile form width;
- confounds: release changes, concurrent A/B variants, member vs new visitor, staff/bot traffic.

Do **not** say a Clarity click implies frustration, or that a heatmap proves causes. Resolve actual defects as baseline repairs, not as a treatment-only A/B change. No sensitive identifiers in analytics.

## Gate 5: proof and objections

Show real proof only with a verified source and right scope. Approval status, accreditation, publication claims and customer ratings are different kinds of evidence. Don't convert ratings into outcome promises; avoid invented endorsements, artificial urgency or buyer quotes. List missing proof as an evidence gap.

## Gate 6: change design and measurement

Every proposed change must have:
- **Before:** existing screenshot/selector/copy, if available.
- **After:** exact proposed change and unchanged protected text/logic.
- **Class:** repair, test, hold or not applicable.
- **Causal hypothesis:** why it may help, explicitly unproven.
- **Measure:** one unit-level primary metric (e.g. valid applications / assigned visitor) plus quality guardrails (e.g. paid customer outcomes and errors), segmentation and exclusion rules.
- **QA:** responsive, functional, accessibility, actual backend handoff, experiment isolation, regressions and publisher signoff.

For A/B proposals, the control and challenger must share the repaired baseline. Do not claim statistical confidence without assigned-user counts and suitable inference. Carry forward evidence limitations and existing holds.

## First barrier prioritization

Rank by (a) verified severity of functional failure, (b) whether it blocks the main action, (c) evidence quality, (d) reach among exposed users, and (e) remediation cost/risk. Do not use fake quantitative scores or invented impact estimates. Give one primary fix and at most three prioritized actions unless user requests exhaustive QA.

## Output style

Use clear headings and a concise evidence table. State if analysis is a *page audit*, *offer readiness* or *offline handoff*. Include source/observation date and commercial approval status. Never convert generic client values into new hardcoded pricing or brand requirements.
